# Output contract

Return JSON matching `shootingScriptSchema` in `src/shared/skill-schemas.ts`.

Return `schemaVersion: shooting-script-v2`. Every ShotSpec must include `physicalPlan` with `schemaVersion: shot-physical-plan-v1`. Relative physical-plan times start at zero for each ShotSpec, camera segments cover the complete duration without gaps or overlap, and every real-space character has at least one explicit body/head/gaze orientation segment.

Every physical plan must also declare `cameraContinuityMode` and `spaceTopology`. Each space has a unique `spaceId`; each doorway, threshold, hatch, or passage that the camera may cross has a unique boundary linking two declared spaces and an explicit `traversalAllowed` value. Every camera segment must declare `spaceId`, `positionAnchor`, `lookAt`, `transitionFromPrevious`, and `boundaryId` (null unless crossing). Every non-initial continuous or boundary-crossing segment must also describe the executable `transitionPath` from the previous position. The first segment is `initial`. In `single-take`, later segments cannot use `cut`; a change of `spaceId` must use `boundary-crossing` through a matching traversable boundary. A `continuous` segment cannot silently move from outside to inside or between disconnected rooms.

Applicability booleans are declarations, not optional hints: a true display/reflection/delayed-state declaration requires corresponding relations or gates; false requires an empty array. Use separate instance IDs for a real subject, its normal reflection, a mirror-only double, and a screen-only participant even when they share one visual asset identity.

For single-sided displays, `user-reading` requires `displayFaces: holder` and the holder in `visibleToInstanceIds`. If the camera must read the screen, use a matching `over-shoulder`, `profile`/`side-angle`, `insert`, or `reflection-view` camera segment. `intentional-presentation` is valid only when the story explicitly changes the interaction to presenting the display to camera.

Every delayed event has a gate with `noEarlyOccurrence: true`; the prose fields must restate the same before/after relationship without changing its time.

Occlusion timing must remain causal: if an occluding surface starts opening at time A and the next space is directly connected, time A is also the first partial reveal. A later time B may be the first clear recognition of a subject, but its `beforeState` cannot claim the directly connected space remains completely hidden. Across shot boundaries, prop hand, height, orientation, character pose/gaze, display state, light state, and reflection state must transfer exactly unless the previous shot explicitly performs and gates the change.

Shot IDs use `S001` form. Timecodes must be continuous, every duration must equal end minus start, and the final end must equal `targetDurationSec`. `startTimeSec`, `endTimeSec`, and `durationSec` must all be whole integers; no decimal production durations are allowed. Each shot must be at least `durationMinSec`, whose product floor is 5 seconds. A 15-second timeline may use 8+7, 9+6, or 5+5+5, but never 7.5+7.5. Use only asset IDs, not ambiguous filenames.

When `generationConstraints` are supplied, each shot duration must be an integer multiple of `durationStepSec`, remain within `durationMinSec` and `durationMaxSec`, the shot count must not exceed `maxShotsForTargetDuration`, and one shot maps to one generation task. Use `segmentationPolicy: content-led-longest-feasible`: combine related beats through blocking and camera movement, prefer durations near `preferredShotDurationSec`, and keep the count near `minimumShotsForTargetDuration` when the dramatic structure permits. A cut needs a real story, staging, state, space/time, or camera-language reason. `avoidDurationPadding: true` forbids adding empty waits, repeated action, or unrelated material merely to reach the preferred duration.

“Longest feasible” is bounded by model executability. Each ShotSpec must remain within `maxMajorBeatsPerShot`, `maxCameraPhasesPerShot`, `maxTimedStateGatesPerShot`, and `maxHighRiskLayersPerShot`. Readable display content, complex reflections, a non-Euclidean/direct-connected space, and a multi-copy crowd each count as one high-risk layer. If all four are required, use a real reveal boundary to split the tasks and transfer the exact end state into the next start state. Camera segments must not exceed the camera-phase budget; exact timed gates are reserved for causally critical first occurrences. Do not encode details that the declared camera cannot see. Copy crowds should normally contain 8–12 readable figures with one collective action, and sound ranges must end at or before their stop/dropout time.
